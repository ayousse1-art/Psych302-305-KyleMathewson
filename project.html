What you may use

Any method from class: simple or choice reaction time, the questionnaire (inventory), a comparison of two conditions, a written methods-and-results page built from files you already have, or a browser game that tests a question (the Week 9 game.html path). A game still needs a one-sentence question, a comparison it records, and a CSV or a result shown on the page.

What you do not need

A partner, classmates’ data, a new code library, or any statistics beyond averages and spreads you can explain. If you want to collect data from classmates, ask the instructor first; the data stay in the room.

Project page 30 points + In-class presentation 5 points = 35

Part A · Project page and FINAL.md · 30 points (30%)

7 points · Working page
A web page (HTML), or a Markdown methods-and-results file with a figure. Someone who is not you can open it and say what you measured.
6 points · Prediction
The prediction from your midterm plan. If your question changed, say so and give the new prediction.
7 points · Result
One figure or table made from your data, and one sentence stating what you found.
5 points · Limitation
One honest limit, including what your amount of data (n) can and cannot show.
3 points · Data file
A CSV in week10-project/ if you collected new data, or the line no new data if you reused an earlier file.
2 points · AI note
What you asked, kept, and changed, or no Copilot tonight.

Part B · In-class presentation · 5 points (5%)

On Wednesday 2 December, open your page in front of the class and walk through it in 3 minutes: your question, your prediction, your result, and your limitation. No slides needed; the page is what you show. Everyone presents. A classmate should be able to repeat your question and your result afterwards.

If you miss the presentation for a reason the instructor accepts, present by Friday 4 December, 17:00. If you hand in neither the page nor the talk, the Faculty process for a missed final assessment applies.

Not marked: how fancy the page looks, extra features, or statistics you cannot explain.

For both parts

Rules that apply to the midterm and the final
