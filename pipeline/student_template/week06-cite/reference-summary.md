## Paper Reference

van den Akker, O. R., van Assen, M. A. L. M., Bakker, M., Elsherif, M., Wong, T. K., & Wicherts, J. M. (2024). Preregistration in practice: A comparison of preregistered and non-preregistered studies in psychology. *Behavior Research Methods, 56*, 5424–5433. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-023-02277-0

## My Summary
The paper examines preregistration, a research practice in which researchers publicly document their research questions, hypotheses, methods, and planned analyses before collecting or analyzing their data.
The main purpose of preregistration is to make psychological research more transparent and trustworthy.
Without preregistration, researchers might unintentionally make decisions after seeing their results that make their findings appear stronger or more convincing. For example, they might test several hypotheses but only report the ones that produce statistically significant results.
Preregistration helps distinguish between analyses researchers planned in advance and analyses they decided to conduct after seeing the data.
The authors investigate how preregistered and non-preregistered psychological studies compare in actual research practice.

### Research Question
The researchers conducted a comparative study of published research, rather than recruiting participants and conducting a new psychological experiment.
They compared:
193 preregistered psychology studies that had received a preregistration badge or award.
193 non-preregistered psychology studies selected as comparable studies.
This gave them a total of 386 studies to examine. They compared the studies on five main outcomes: positive results, effect sizes, statistical inconsistencies, use of power analyses, and sample sizes. They also explored publication time and indicators of scientific impact.

### Methods
The researchers conducted a comparative study of published research, rather than recruiting participants and conducting a new psychological experiment.
They compared:
193 preregistered psychology studies that had received a preregistration badge or award.
193 non-preregistered psychology studies selected as comparable studies.
This gave them a total of 386 studies to examine. They compared the studies on five main outcomes: positive results, effect sizes, statistical inconsistencies, use of power analyses, and sample sizes. They also explored publication time and indicators of scientific impact.



### Main Findings
- Positive results: 69% of preregistered studies and 68% of non-preregistered studies had positive results.
- Effect sizes: The difference between the two groups was not statistically significant.
- Statistical inconsistencies: Preregistration did not show a clear reduction in statistical inconsistencies.
- Power analyses: 55% of preregistered studies used power analyses, compared with 23% of non-preregistered studies.
- Sample sizes: The median sample size was 216 for preregistered studies and 116 for non-preregistered studies.
- Publication and impact: Preregistration was not associated with significantly longer publication times, and preregistered studies scored higher on several measures of scientific impact.
### Conclusion
The authors conclude that preregistration has practical benefits for psychological research, particularly regarding statistical planning and sample size. However, their findings do not establish that preregistration alone prevents questionable research practices or produces fewer positive results.
The key lesson is that preregistration is a useful transparency tool, but it is not a substitute for sound research design, adequate statistical power, and honest reporting.

### My Reflection
[I learned that preregistration is a research method that involves documenting research questions, hypotheses, and planned analyses before collecting or analyzing data. It helps increase transparency and reduces the risk of researchers selectively reporting results or changing hypotheses after seeing the data. However, I also learned that preregistration does not automatically eliminate bias or guarantee high-quality research, so it should be combined with sound research design and appropriate statistical methods.]
